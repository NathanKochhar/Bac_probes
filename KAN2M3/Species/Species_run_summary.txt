KAN2M3 SPECIES PROBE DESIGN - RUN SUMMARY (WITH BLAST)
======================================================
Date: 2026-09-29
Database: SILVA 138.1 SSURef NR99 (510,508 sequences) + SILVA BLAST database
Probe design: 32-mers, GC 35-65%, no homopolymers >= 5
BLAST: blastn-short, >= 85% identity, max_target_seqs 50,000
Machine: AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D (8 cores / 16 threads), 31 GB RAM

The "probe" column in every output file is the reverse complement of the k-mer;
this is the sequence to order for in-situ use.


HEADLINE
--------
Only 2 of 7 species reach the ~50% pooled-coverage goal with highly specific
probes: Helicobacter pylori (86.7%) and Fusobacterium nucleatum (55.5%).
Cutibacterium acnes gets 2 probes (7.6%). E. coli, C. jejuni, E. hormaechei and
P. jejuni get none: their 16S sequences are too close to named sister species
for any 32-mer to be specific. This is a known limit of 16S rRNA at species
level, not a parameter problem.


KEY METRICS
-----------
exact_precision = target hits / (target hits + exact off-target hits)

blast_weighted_specificity (used for selection)
    = BLAST target hits / (target hits + weighted off-target hits)
    Each off-target BLAST hit (>= 85% identity) is weighted by how likely it
    is to still bind: an exact match counts fully; mismatches near the probe
    centre count ~0 (won't bind); mismatches near the ends count nearly fully.


SPECIES DEFINITION IN SILVA (important)
---------------------------------------
SILVA's species field is the raw organism name, so it was handled as follows:

1. Strains and subspecies are merged: every label is cut to its first two words
   ("Escherichia coli O157:H7" -> "Escherichia coli",
    "Enterobacter hormaechei subsp. oharae" -> "Enterobacter hormaechei").

2. Every SILVA sequence is classified relative to each target species:
     target    - labelled with the target species
     ambiguous - same SILVA genus as the target but no real species name
                 ("Fusobacterium sp.", "uncultured bacterium",
                  "uncultured Helicobacter sp." ...). These could be the target
                 itself, so they are EXCLUDED from both target and off-target
                 counts.
     off       - everything else, including every NAMED sister species
                 (e.g. Shigella, Campylobacter coli, Fusobacterium periodonticum).
   The same rule is used for exact counting and for BLAST hit classification.

   A first run without the "ambiguous" class counted unnamed same-genus
   sequences as off-target. That made almost every probe look non-specific
   (the top "off-target" was usually "uncultured bacterium"), so the run was
   repeated with this rule.

GOAL
----
~10 highly specific probes per species with POOLED coverage of ~50%.
Individual probes may have low coverage if they are very specific.


METHOD
------
species_pipeline.py is the family pipeline adapted for species (same k-mer
filters, exact counting and position-weighted BLAST scoring as bac-probes).

  - Stage 1 (exact): candidates are k-mers present in >= 2% of the species'
    sequences, with exact off-target counts across SILVA.
  - Stage 2 (BLAST): up to 120 candidates per species at distinct sites
    (no shared 16-bp stretch) with exact precision >= 0.90. Only 108 qualified
    in total (0 for E. hormaechei).
  - Selection: floors tried on blast_weighted_specificity from strict to loose
    (0.999, 0.995, 0.99, 0.98, 0.95, 0.90). The strictest floor whose pool
    reaches 50% pooled coverage is used. Greedy picks maximise new target
    sequences covered, then extra non-overlapping probes at the same floor fill
    remaining slots, up to 10.
  - The whole run took ~10 minutes.

H. pylori has only 6 probes because no further non-overlapping probes passed
the 0.999 floor. All 6 have zero exact off-targets.

F. nucleatum needed the 0.95 floor. Its main cross-reactor is F. periodonticum,
a close oral relative; 5 of the 8 probes have zero exact off-targets.


WHY THE OTHER SPECIES FAILED
----------------------------
  Escherichia coli
    E. coli is known to be indistinguishable from Shigella by 16S, and
    Salmonella is very close. Best BLAST-scored candidate: 0.898 weighted
    specificity at 35% coverage, top off-target Salmonella enterica. No
    candidate reached 0.90.

  Campylobacter jejuni
    Near-identical 16S to C. coli. Best candidates: 0.896 and 0.869 weighted
    specificity, just below the 0.90 floor.

  Enterobacter hormaechei
    Member of the E. cloacae complex, whose species are known to share
    nearly identical 16S. No k-mer reached even 0.90 exact precision (best 0.67), so
    nothing was BLASTed.

  Prevotella jejuni
    Only 5 sequences in SILVA. Its best k-mer has 28 near-miss off-target
    BLAST hits (weighted 4.0, top: P. denticola) against only 5 target hits,
    giving 0.56 weighted specificity; all others are lower.

  Cutibacterium acnes
    Only 79 sequences are named C. acnes, while 1,585 Cutibacterium sequences
    have no species name. Few k-mers look acnes-specific, and those that do
    cover only 2-5% each. Many of the 1,585 unnamed sequences are probably
    C. acnes, so real coverage may be higher than 7.6%, but SILVA can't
    confirm it.

The best near-miss candidates for every species are in
find_runs/<Species>_blast_scored.tsv (local only) if you want to accept lower
specificity for any of them.

Options for these species if species-level detection is required:
  - Accept genus- or family-level probes (see ../Genus and ../Family).
  - Use a non-16S marker gene (e.g. rpoB, gyrB, groEL) or species-specific
    genes, where sister species differ far more.

CAVEATS
-------
  - Species labels in SILVA are incomplete and sometimes wrong; results depend
    on them more than at any other level.
  - Only the BLAST pool (up to 120 candidates per species) was BLAST-scored.
  - Specificity is measured against SILVA 138.1 only.
