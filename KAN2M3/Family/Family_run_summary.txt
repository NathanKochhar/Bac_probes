KAN2M3 FAMILY PROBE DESIGN - RUN SUMMARY (WITH BLAST)
=====================================================
Date: 2026-09-29
Database: SILVA 138.1 SSURef NR99 (510,508 sequences) + SILVA BLAST database
Probe design: 32-mers, GC 35-65%, no homopolymers >= 5
BLAST: blastn-short, >= 85% identity, max_target_seqs 50,000
Machine: AMD Ryzen 7 9800X3D (8 cores / 16 threads), 31 GB RAM

The "probe" column in every output file is the reverse complement of the k-mer;
this is the sequence to order for in-situ use.


KEY METRICS
-----------
exact_precision = target hits / (target hits + exact off-target hits)
    Fraction of SILVA sequences carrying the exact 32-mer that belong to the
    family. 1.0 = zero exact off-target matches.

blast_weighted_specificity (used for selection)
    = BLAST target hits / (target hits + weighted off-target hits)
    Every off-target BLAST hit (>= 85% identity, i.e. up to ~4 mismatches) is
    weighted by how likely it is to still bind:
      - exact match -> weight 1 (will bind)
      - mismatches near the probe centre -> weight ~0 (won't bind)
      - mismatches near the probe ends -> weight near 1 (may still bind)
    So this captures near-miss cross-reactivity that exact matching misses.

blast_offtarget_hits (raw) is large for every probe (thousands) because 16S is
highly conserved and 85% identity admits hits with several mismatches. Almost
all of these carry centrally placed mismatches and contribute ~0 to the
weighted score; use the weighted specificity, not the raw count.


NAME MAPPING
------------
All 25 family names in KAN2M3-family.csv match SILVA 138.1 exactly.


GOAL
----
~10 highly specific probes per family with POOLED coverage of ~50%.
Individual probes may have low coverage if they are very specific.


METHOD
------
family_pipeline.py reuses the bac_probes library (same k-mer filters, exact
off-target counting and BLAST scoring as `bac-probes find --blast`), but reads
SILVA once for all 25 families instead of 50 full passes.

Stage 1 - exact (~12 min, 14 threads)
  - Candidates: k-mers present in >= 2% of the family's sequences.
  - Exact off-target counts against all other SILVA sequences.

Stage 2 - BLAST (~45 min, 2,714 queries, ~1.3 s each)
  - BLASTing every candidate (3,000-11,000 per family) is not feasible, so
    each family BLASTs a pool of up to 120 candidates at distinct sites
    (no two share a 16-bp stretch): up to 60 chosen for coverage and 60 for
    exact precision, all with exact precision >= 0.90.

Selection
  - Specificity floors tried from strict to loose on blast_weighted_specificity:
    0.999, 0.995, 0.99, 0.98, 0.95, 0.90. The strictest floor whose
    10-probe pool reaches 50% pooled coverage is used.
  - Greedy: each pick adds the most not-yet-covered target sequences; probes
    sharing a 16-bp stretch are skipped.
  - Top-up: if coverage saturates before 10 probes, the remaining slots are
    filled with extra non-overlapping probes at the same floor.

Summary
  - All 25 families reach the ~50% pooled-coverage goal (53-91%).
  - Every pool has BLAST weighted specificity >= 0.98; 24 of 25 are >= 0.99
    and 10 are >= 0.999.
  - 7 families have zero exact off-targets across all selected probes.
  - No selected probe hit the BLAST max_target_seqs cap, so the BLAST
    specificity estimates are not truncated.
  - Enterococcaceae has 9 probes, not 10: no further non-overlapping probe
    passed the 0.995 floor.
  - Weakest pools: Lachnospiraceae (floor 0.98) and Ruminococcaceae /
    Tannerellaceae / Rikenellaceae (floor 0.99). These sit in crowded
    lineages (Lachnospirales/Oscillospirales, Bacteroidales); their main
    cross-reactors are sister families.

CAVEATS
-------
  - Only the BLAST pool (up to 120 candidates per family) was BLAST-scored.
    Other candidates could exist that were never considered.
  - Specificity is measured against SILVA 138.1 only. Taxa under-represented
    in SILVA may behave differently in real samples.
